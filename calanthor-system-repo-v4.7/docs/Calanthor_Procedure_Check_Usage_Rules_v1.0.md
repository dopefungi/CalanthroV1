# Calanthor Procedure Check Usage Rules (v1.0)
*DC-based, D&D-native, and built to stay fun even with bad luck.*

These rules govern **when** to call a Procedure Check, **how often**, and **what failure means** so the table doesn’t stall or spiral when the dice run cold.

---

## 1) What a Procedure Check is
A **Procedure Check** is a *named, repeatable* D&D check used for **travel, hazards, rites, negotiation, and survival logistics**.

It is still just:
> **d20 + ability mod + proficiency (if applicable)** vs a **DC**

Procedure Checks exist to:
- make regions feel mechanically distinct
- create interesting costs/choices
- avoid “roll spam” and dead ends

---

## 2) When to call a Procedure Check (the 3-question gate)
Only call a Procedure Check if **all three** are true:

1) **Uncertain outcome:** success is not guaranteed *and* failure is plausible.
2) **Meaningful stakes:** the result changes the situation (time, safety, resources, position, attention).
3) **Actionable consequence:** on failure, something happens besides “you don’t do it.”

If any answer is “no,” **do not roll**. Narrate success or use a simple time cost.

---

## 3) How often to roll (anti-roll-spam rules)
### One roll per obstacle
An “obstacle” is a meaningful barrier: a stormfront, a ravine, a negotiation, a purification rite, a day of tundra travel.

- **Don’t** roll for every step, every minute, every door.
- **Do** roll once for the obstacle, then narrate the outcome across the whole beat.

### Travel cadence (recommended)
For overland travel, roll **at most once per travel leg**:
- per **watch** (about 4 hours), or
- per **major terrain shift** (storm hits / canyon begins / mire deepens)

### Encounter cadence (recommended)
During a combat encounter, call **0–1 Procedure Checks** total unless the whole encounter is built around the hazard (bridge collapse / veilstorm / ritual under fire).

---

## 4) Before you roll: set stakes and “retry cost”
Always say (out loud) three things:
- **Intent:** what you want
- **Approach:** how you do it (skill/tool/spell)
- **Stakes:** what happens on failure

Also decide:
- **Can they retry?**
  - If yes, what’s the cost (time, supplies, attention, strain)?
  - If no, why not (storm window closes, bridge collapses, guards arrive)?

This prevents “we roll forever until we succeed.”

---

## 5) Failure rules that keep the game fun (Fail Forward)
A Procedure Check failure should **not** stop play. It should change play.

### Default failure menu (pick 1, sometimes 2 on fail-by-5)
On failure, choose one consequence type:
1) **Time:** you succeed too late / lose a travel leg / the window closes
2) **Supplies:** lose rations, arrows, rope, medicine, shelter materials
3) **Position:** you arrive in a worse place (split, exposed, low ground, wrong side of the ravine)
4) **Attention:** you attract a threat (predator, patrol, omen, rival faction)
5) **Strain:** one character gains **Minor Strain**
6) **Domain Pressure:** if a domain overlay is active, its track increases by **+1**

**Important:** You can still allow progress (see “Assured Progress,” below).

---

## 6) The “Bad Luck Protection” package (choose what you like)
These are optional but strongly recommended. They preserve tension without punishing unlucky streaks.

### A) Assured Progress (recommended default)
If the party fails the **same obstacle** twice, the third attempt cannot be a dead end.

Choose one:
- **Succeed with a cost** (automatic success, but take a consequence from the menu), or
- **Reduce the DC by 5**, or
- **Grant advantage** on the next attempt

This protects pacing while keeping consequences meaningful.

### B) Push for Success (player choice)
After seeing the roll but before the outcome is narrated, a player may say **“I Push.”**
They turn a failure into success, but must accept **two** costs from the failure menu
(or one cost + domain track +1 if an overlay is active).

### C) Complication Cap (scene safety valve)
In a single scene/obstacle, only **one major complication** can occur from Procedure Checks.
Additional failures become **minor complications** (flavor strain, small supply loss, mild position shift).

This stops spirals where 3 bad rolls erase a session’s momentum.

### D) Help Always Helps (anti-feels-bad rule)
If an ally meaningfully helps and is proficient (or uses a relevant tool/spell), assistance grants:
- **advantage**, OR
- **+2** to the roll (choose at table)

This makes teamwork reliable and reduces “one bad roller ruins travel.”

---

## 7) “Take 10” and “Buy Success” options (D&D-friendly)
### Take 10 (no pressure)
If the party has time and safety (no chase, no storm timer), they can **Take 10**:
- treat the d20 as a 10
- still pay time (GM sets)

### Buy Success (spend resources to reduce uncertainty)
Before rolling, players can spend to improve odds:
- **1 supply** (rations/rope/medicine): +2
- **a spell slot** relevant to the task: advantage
- **Ritecraft kit use** with materials: reduce DC by 2–5 (GM)

This turns the system into choices, not roulette.

---

## 8) Group checks (travel and hazards)
To smooth variance, use **group checks** for travel:
- Everyone rolls.
- If **at least half** succeed, the party succeeds.
- If fewer succeed, apply failure (but consider “Assured Progress” so you still move).

Or use **leader + support**:
- One leader rolls.
- Up to two supporters grant +2 each if they explain how they help (max +4).

---

## 9) Domain Pressure interaction (important)
Domain overlays are usually **OFF**.
When ON:
- failure menu may include **Domain Track +1**
- success-by-5 may reduce the track by **-1** (if fictionally appropriate)
- the track should create **choices**, not lockouts:
  - shelter exists
  - rites exist
  - bargains exist
  - detours exist

If the track hits 6, it should trigger a dramatic event—not “you can’t play.”

---

## 10) Simple DC calibration for Calanthor regions
- **Normal region travel:** DC 15
- **Hostile subzone travel:** DC 20
- **Mythic/sacred site:** DC 20–25
- **Prepared route / skilled guide / proper rite:** lower DC by 2–5 or grant advantage

Examples:
- Frozen Tundra whiteout: DC 20 (DC 15 with a Whisperer guide + cairn rite)
- Cracked Earth aimless crossing: DC 20; with declared purpose + Ashwalker chant: DC 15
- Deep Mire crossing: DC 20–25; with web-lane map + antitoxin: DC 20

---

## 11) One-page GM checklist (fast use at table)
Before calling a Procedure Check:
- Is it uncertain? stakes? actionable consequence?
- What’s the obstacle scope (one roll, not many)?
- What happens on failure (choose 1 consequence)?
- Is Assured Progress active if they fail twice?
- Can players Push, Take 10, or Buy Success?

---

### Recommended default configuration (if you want the “best feel”)
Turn ON:
- Assured Progress
- Help Always Helps
- Group checks for travel
- Buy Success

Keep optional:
- Complication Cap (use in swingy groups)
- Take 10 (only when no timers)
